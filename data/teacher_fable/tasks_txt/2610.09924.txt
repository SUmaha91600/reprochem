The success of the BSE comes from a screened Coulomb interaction that explicitly accounts for interactions between electrons and holes.[37–43] The BSE has become a benchmark approach for molecular and condensed-phase spectroscopy, but generating the screened Coulomb matrix elements scales quartically with system size. This has limited application to large-scale molecular aggregate settings.[40–49] As a result, a large gap persists between experimentally relevant molecular aggregates and the many-body methods needed to describe them. Our recent developments in stochastic many-body theory provide a route toward closing this gap.[50–57] By replacing the explicit construction of the real-space 3 screened Coulomb operator with stochastic realizations of the operator’s action, these approaches retain the essential many-body physics of the BSE while substantially reducing computational cost. Previous work has shown that stochastic screened-interaction methods reproduce BSE-quality excitation energies for challenging polymethine cyanine dyes while extending excited-state calculations to systems containing several thousand valence electrons.[58–61] In this work, we apply _ab initio_ stochastic Bethe-Salpeter Equation (sBSE), also known as TDHF@ _vW_ ,[58, 60, 61] to aggregate clusters (containing up to 2,856 valence electrons) of 4<sup>_′_</sup> -(3,5dimethylphenyl)-substituted heptamethine cyanine dye (Cy7-DiMePh).[7, 9, 10, 62]
